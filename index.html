About

I'm a gameplay and systems programmer in Tel Aviv.

At Gliding Deer I head client development for mobile games in Unity, owning the client end to end. I took over an existing live title and brought it onto a newer Unity version, improved its runtime performance and rebuilt its build pipeline. Most of my work now is on new prototypes, taking them from first playable toward something releasable. I also build the AI-assisted tooling we develop with — a multi-agent pipeline that generates Unity scenes, assets and gameplay scaffolding.

Before that I spent two years at Communix on Chip Master, a live real-stakes multiplayer poker game shipped to Unity WebGL. Browser WASM is an unforgiving target: a fixed heap the browser enforces, no swap, no platform SDK, and a download size that decides whether a player ever reaches the first frame. Working inside those limits taught me more about memory and frame budgets than anything else I have done.

Before games I spent three years at Check Point writing production C and C++ for high-throughput gateway systems, in a multi-million-line codebase where a fault takes down customer traffic. That is where I learned to debug what only fails under load. I hold a BSc in Computer Science from Bar Ilan University.

I'm looking to work on large-scale games abroad and I'm open to relocation. Outside work I'm drawn to exploring different cultures, and to tabletop RPGs, where most of my design thinking comes from.
